## Business Model

Lawn Love is a **tech-enabled lawn care managed marketplace** — it dispatches a single provider per job (not lead-resale) and uses satellite imagery + GPS for automated yard measurement and routing. The consumer pays Lawn Love directly; providers are paid by the platform.

### Revenue Structure
- Consumer pays Lawn Love at a Lawn Love-set price (based on satellite-measured yard size)
- Lawn Love dispatches one provider per job
- Platform takes **15–20% commission** from provider earnings
- Provider receives the remainder

### Consumer Pricing Model
- **Instant quotes** generated from satellite imagery of the property (no in-person estimate required)
- Pricing is based on measured yard square footage
- Example pricing cited in consumer complaints: ~$55.99 biweekly mowing plan
- Recurring plans are the primary offering (biweekly, monthly, seasonal)

---

## Key Technology Differentiator

**Satellite-based yard measurement:**
- Lawn Love uses satellite imagery (Google Maps/similar API) to automatically measure yard square footage without requiring an in-person visit
- Combined with GPS routing, this enables fully automated job dispatch and pricing
- This is Lawn Love's primary tech moat — no quoting call required

**Provider routing algorithm:** Jobs are assigned to providers based on location, availability, and service capacity — similar to a ride-share routing model applied to lawn care.

## Lead Resale: NO (Managed Dispatch)
Lawn Love dispatches a single provider per job — not a lead marketplace. The consumer doesn't select a provider; Lawn Love's algorithm assigns one.

## Payout Speed: Unclear / Disputed
No publicly stated payout timeline. Multiple provider complaints report:
- Jobs completed with no payment received
- Weeks or months of follow-up required to receive owed funds
- Some providers report taking Lawn Love to small claims court to collect payment

## Recurring Bookings: YES (Core Feature)
Recurring lawn care is the primary product. Biweekly and monthly plans are the default offering. Seasonal plans (spring cleanup, fall aeration, snow removal) supplement recurring revenue.

## Quality Verification Beyond Stars: Limited
- Background checks implied but not prominently advertised
- No structured job-completion verification
- No composite quality scoring
- Consumer reviews only (on Trustpilot, Google, BBB)

## Crew/Team Accounts: NO
Individual provider accounts only. No crew management.

## Competitive Position vs. Alternatives

**GreenPal** (lawncare marketplace competitor) explicitly publishes comparison content criticizing Lawn Love's commission rates as "high." GreenPal operates on a lower-commission model and positions itself as provider-friendly. This competitive dynamic suggests Lawn Love's 15–20% take rate is above market for managed lawn care.

## HomeBase Strategic Implications

1. **Lawn Love proves the recurring lawn care subscription model works.** Consumers will sign up for automated recurring lawn service — the demand is validated. HomeBase's MVP-1 (subscription plans for lawn + cleaning) enters an established market.
2. **Lawn Love's execution failures are HomeBase's acquisition opportunity.** Provider non-payment, billing errors, and poor customer service are the specific pain points HomeBase solves with verified-completion-before-payout and transparent billing.
3. **Lawn Love's commission rate (15–20%) creates price compression opportunity.** HomeBase's 10% take on subscription bookings is materially lower, creating a compelling provider value proposition in direct comparison.
4. **Satellite measurement is a feature, not a moat.** Lawn Love's satellite-based quoting is useful but replicable via Google Maps API. HomeBase can add automated yard measurement without building proprietary satellite infrastructure.
5. **Geographic gaps in western U.S.** Lawn Love is thin west of the Mississippi in many markets. HomeBase's Austin beachhead is in a Lawn Love-covered market — this is a direct competitive test market.
