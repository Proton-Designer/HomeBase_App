# HomeBase — Competitors Overview

> **Purpose:** High-level competitive briefing for all agents. Covers the five main consumer marketplace competitors and two B2B FSM tools. Read this to understand the competitive landscape and why HomeBase's model wins. For deep dives, see `docs/context/competitors/[COMPETITOR].md`.
> **Last updated:** 2026-06-06 (based on June 2026 research)

## Market Context

The U.S. home services market is valued at ~$97B (2025), growing at 7.2% CAGR toward $195B by 2035. The dominant platforms share a critical structural weakness HomeBase is built to exploit: **pay-per-lead** — charging providers the moment a homeowner contacts them, regardless of whether the job happens, creating a race-to-call rather than a race-to-quality.

## The Five Consumer Marketplace Competitors

### 1. Angi (angi.com)
**Parent:** Independent public company (spun off from IAC, April 2025, NASDAQ: ANGI)

Angi is the largest home services marketplace by revenue (~$1.19B in 2024, though declining 13% YoY). It operates three models: **Angi Leads** (pay-per-lead sold to 3–8 contractors simultaneously), **Angi Ads** (pay-per-click display), and **Angi Services** (managed dispatch, acquired Handy in 2018). The platform earns on the **inquiry**, not the job.

**Key weaknesses:** FTC ordered $7.2M in fines (2023) for deceptive lead marketing. Active provider count down 17% YoY. Annual lock-in contracts with 35% early termination fees. BBB "Pattern of Complaints" flagged. Network Revenue declined 79% YoY in Q4 2025 as the company tried to pivot away from lead-resale — proving the model is structurally failing.

**HomeBase advantage:** HomeBase's completion-based model is literally incompatible with Angi's revenue model. Angi cannot adopt it without destroying their P&L.

---

### 2. Thumbtack (thumbtack.com)
**Status:** Private, $3.2B valuation, $699M raised

Thumbtack covers 1,100+ service categories, earning ~$400M in revenue in 2024 (+27% YoY — the strongest growth in the category). Business model is pay-per-lead, sold to 4–5 pros simultaneously. Lead costs are dynamically priced weekly, making provider ROI unpredictable. Providers report paying $800+/month with conversion rates dropping from 80% to 20% over two years.

**Recent moves:** Became one of the first apps inside ChatGPT (OpenAI Operator, Jan 2025). Also integrated with Amazon Alexa+ for voice booking (Feb 2025). These AI distribution deals are the real competitive threat — not their core product.

**Key weaknesses:** No lead exclusivity; dynamic pricing opacity; no quality verification beyond background checks; no crew/team accounts.

**HomeBase advantage:** Thumbtack's AI distribution is a real long-term threat for discovery. HomeBase must build AI-native discovery or partner early. On the provider value proposition, Thumbtack's pay-per-lead model remains a structural weakness — HomeBase providers never pay to quote, only when they win and complete a job.

---

### 3. TaskRabbit (taskrabbit.com)
**Owner:** IKEA Group (acquired 2017)

TaskRabbit covers on-demand tasks in all 50 U.S. states + 8 countries. Revenue ~$75M. The consumer selects a specific Tasker (not a lead blast), which makes it the most similar to HomeBase's philosophy among incumbents. Charges 15% service fee on providers + 5–15% Trust & Support fee on consumers. Total platform take can reach 30% of job value.

**IKEA integration:** TaskRabbit assembly can now be booked within IKEA's checkout flow — powerful distribution for furniture assembly specifically.

**Key weaknesses:** No quality verification beyond background checks. No crew accounts. No structured trust scoring. High fees (15% provider + consumer fee). Revenue small relative to scale. Declining consumer satisfaction.

**HomeBase advantage:** TaskRabbit's consumer-picks-specific-Tasker model gives homeowner choice, similar to HomeBase, but TaskRabbit charges a 15% provider fee and lacks the completion-verified trust layer that makes the choice meaningful. HomeBase providers pay nothing unless they win and complete the job.

---

### 4. Handy / Angi Services (handy.com → angi.com/services)
**Owner:** Angi Inc. (acquired October 2018, ~$165.5M stock)

Handy operates as a managed dispatch marketplace for cleaning and handyman — dispatching a single provider per job (fixed-price, no bidding). Consumer books at a flat rate; Handy takes an undisclosed margin; worker receives the remainder. Core feature: recurring cleaning subscriptions (weekly/biweekly/monthly).

**Key weaknesses:** Severe regulatory exposure — FTC + NY AG filed complaint in January 2025 resulting in $2.95M settlement for deceiving workers about earnings. California: $6M misclassification settlement (2023). Default payout is 7 days (triggered FTC complaint over "daily pay" advertising). Workers report $50 fines levied even for consumer-initiated cancellations.

**HomeBase advantage:** Handy's single-dispatch model is directionally correct, but their worker treatment is a regulatory and reputational liability. A platform with transparent, faster payouts and no punitive fee system directly captures disgruntled Handy providers.

---

### 5. Lawn Love (lawnlove.com)
**Status:** Private

Lawn Love is a tech-enabled lawn care marketplace (managed dispatch, single provider per job). Uses satellite imagery + GPS for yard measurement. Core offering is recurring lawn care plans. Platform takes 15–20% commission on completed jobs.

**Key weaknesses:** Multiple documented provider non-payment complaints (some went to small claims court). Consumer billing without service delivery documented. Geographic gaps (western U.S. thin coverage). Higher commissions than competitors like GreenPal.

**HomeBase advantage:** Lawn Love's recurring model is sound; execution on payment reliability and provider communication is severely flawed. HomeBase's verified-completion-before-payout model directly addresses their primary weakness.

## The Two B2B FSM Competitors

These are operational software tools, not lead marketplaces. They solve the "run my business" problem but not the "get me customers" problem.

### 6. Housecall Pro (housecallpro.com)
SaaS FSM for home service businesses. Pricing: $59–$189+/mo (annually). Best for 1–8 tech teams needing scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, GPS tracking, and customer communication. Strong real-time dispatching and Recurring Service Plans module. No lead generation capability.

**Relationship to HomeBase:** A Housecall Pro user still needs leads. HomeBase is complementary at MVP; direct competition when the HomeBase OS ships.

### 7. Jobber (getjobber.com)
SaaS FSM for home service businesses. Pricing: $39–$599+/mo (annually) depending on team size. Strongest CRM and client management of any FSM tool. Better QuickBooks integration than HCP. Add-on AI Receptionist ($99/mo) and Marketing Suite ($79/mo). No lead generation capability.

**Relationship to HomeBase:** Same as Housecall Pro — complementary at MVP. HomeBase OS at $349/mo flat targets operators who want one platform instead of paying for Jobber + lead-gen separately.

## What This Means for Every Agent

1. **Never charge providers for leads, quotes, or contacts.** Angi and Thumbtack prove the pay-per-lead model is in structural decline and regulatory crosshairs. HomeBase's completion-based revenue is the differentiator — providers only pay when they get paid.
2. **The check-in widget is the moat.** No competitor has structured job-completion verification. This is white space — protect it.
3. **Payout speed matters.** Handy's 7-day default triggered federal enforcement. HomeBase's same-day Stripe Instant payout is a hard competitive advantage — never weaken it.
4. **Thumbtack's AI distribution is the real threat.** If Thumbtack (or Angi) becomes the default AI assistant answer for home services, volume covers their non-exclusive lead problem. HomeBase needs AI-native discovery.
5. **Crew/team support is a gap no consumer platform fills.** All five consumer marketplaces are solo-operator only. HomeBase's crew/team accounts (MVP-10) serve a segment no marketplace competitor currently addresses.
